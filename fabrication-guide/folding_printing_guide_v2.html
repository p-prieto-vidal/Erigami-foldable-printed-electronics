01 Universal print rules apply to all 6 patterns
Rule Value / Instruction Why it matters
Trace orientation Always parallel to the fold axis — never perpendicular across a crease Perpendicular traces crack open at 90–180°, causing open circuit
Trace clearance from crease Minimum 2 mm from any crease line to nearest trace edge Crease region has maximum stress — ink delaminates if trace is too close
Trace width 0.5 mm standard · 1.0 mm for patterns with >2 creases Narrower = more brittle at crease. Wider = more robust but higher R baseline
Ink passes 1 pass for all specimens (matches your calibrated Ceramics V9 profile) Consistent baseline. Add a 2-pass variant only in Week 4 if time allows
Contact pads 3 × 3 mm pads at each trace end, positioned on the flat face, not on a crease Pads are where you place multimeter probes — must be in a non-fold zone
Crease pre-scoring Score creases with a blunt stylus before printing Controlled crease radius reduces unpredictable ink stress at fold
Kirigami cuts Cut with craft knife after printing and curing Cutting before printing shifts print registration off the trace
Curing Ceramics V9 profile: 130 °C, air dry, standard curing time Under-cured ink cracks earlier; over-cured makes paper brittle
Measurement timing Take R reading within 30 seconds of folding Paper has viscoelastic recovery — R changes if you wait longer
02 Pattern 1 — Single crease Week 1 · Baseline
01 · W1
Single crease — parallel trace
BASELINE SPECIMEN A · essential reference for all other patterns
Paper & print layout
crease
trace (0.5 mm)
2 mm clear zone
100 × 80 mm trace: parallel width: 0.5 mm 2 traces / specimen
Print & fold steps
  1. 1. Cut paper to 100 × 80 mm. Landscape orientation (100 mm wide).
  2. 2. Score crease at the horizontal centreline (40 mm from each edge) with a blunt stylus and ruler. Score on the back face.
  3. 3. Clamp flat to the Voltera bed. Probe-map the surface.
  4. 4. Print two traces running left to right (parallel to the crease) — one 30 mm above the crease, one 30 mm below. 3 × 3 mm contact pads at each end.
  5. 5. Cure with Ceramics V9 profile. Let cool completely before folding.
  6. 6. Measure R0 flat. Then fold to 90°, measure. Fold to 180°, measure. Unfold, measure again (recovery).
  7. 7. Repeat fold–unfold for 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 cycles, measuring R after each checkpoint.
! Also print a perpendicular control specimen with the trace running perpendicular (top to bottom, crossing the crease) — this corresponds to SC_90_L1/L2 in the dataset. It provides a failure reference: high ΔR/R₀ from the first fold cycles.

03 Angle study — 0° / 45° / 90° variants Week 1–2 · cross-pattern sub-study
→ The angle study adds 6 extra specimens across three patterns. Each tests whether the ΔR/R₀ relationship with trace angle is gradual (a smooth curve) or has a sharp threshold. This is novel data for your specific ink + paper + printer combination — not covered in existing literature.
SC · W1
Single crease — 0° / 45° / 90°
3 specimens · same paper, same crease, only trace angle changes
Three layouts — 100 × 80 mm each
100 × 80 mm × 3 same Gerber file, rotate trace only
Print instructions
  1. 1. Cut 3 identical sheets of 100 × 80 mm. Score crease at horizontal centre on all 3.
  2. 2. SC_0 (0°): print trace horizontally (parallel to crease). Corresponds to SC_0_L1 / SC_0_L2 in the dataset.
  3. 3. EXP-SC-45: rotate the trace 45° in the Gerber file. The trace runs diagonally from one corner, crossing the crease at 45°. Keep contact pads on the flat faces above and below the crease, not at the crossing point.
  4. 4. EXP-SC-90: print trace vertically (90° — perpendicular to crease). Single vertical line crossing the crease at its midpoint.
  5. 5. Cure all 3 together. Fold all to 180°. Measure ΔR/R₀ for each immediately.
  6. 6. Cycle all 3 to 100 cycles. The shape of the ΔR curve across the three specimens is your key finding.
! For the 45° specimen, the contact pads must sit on the flat triangular face areas — not on the crease. Place one pad in the top-left zone and one in the bottom-right zone of the paper.

ACC · W2
Parallel accordion — 0° / 45° / 90°
3 specimens · 4 creases each · tests cumulative angle stress across multiple creases
Three layouts — 100 × 100 mm each
100 × 100 mm × 3 4 vertical creases each
Print instructions & key insight
  1. 1. Cut 3 sheets of 100 × 100 mm. Score 4 vertical creases at 20/40/60/80 mm on all 3.
  2. 2. EXP-ACC-00: print one vertical trace centred on each panel face (5 panels = 5 traces). Connect with short bridges at alternating top/bottom edges. This is the standard accordion baseline.
  3. 3. EXP-ACC-45: print a single diagonal trace running from top-left to bottom-right across the full sheet, crossing all 4 creases at ~45°. Contact pads at top-left and bottom-right corners of the sheet.
  4. 4. EXP-ACC-90: print a single horizontal trace at the midline of the sheet, crossing all 4 creases perpendicularly. Contact pads at left and right edges.
  5. 5. The key comparison is: does the 45° accumulate stress linearly across 4 crossings, or does the oblique angle distribute stress better than 4 perpendicular crossings?
→ The 45° accordion specimen is your most interesting data point. A single diagonal trace hits all 4 creases — if it survives better than the 90° specimen despite crossing all creases, it confirms that angle distribution matters more than avoiding crossings entirely.

MO · W2
Miura-ori — horizontal / 45° / vertical on panels
3 specimens · Miura has two crease directions simultaneously — angle means something different here
The Miura difference
→ Unlike single crease or accordion, Miura-ori has two crease directions at once — horizontal rows and offset vertical columns. So no trace orientation is purely "parallel" to everything. Instead, test three positions on the panel face to find which direction survives best.
110 × 90 mm × 3 2 mm clear from ALL crease directions
Print instructions
  1. 1. Cut 3 sheets of 110 × 90 mm. Score the same Miura crease pattern on all 3.
  2. 2. EXP-MO-H: print traces horizontally on each panel face, parallel to the horizontal creases. This is the standard Miura baseline from the literature.
  3. 3. EXP-MO-45: print traces diagonally across each panel face at ~45°. The trace will be oblique to both the horizontal creases and the offset vertical hinges.
  4. 4. EXP-MO-90: print traces vertically on each panel face. Vertical traces are perpendicular to the horizontal creases but run roughly parallel to the vertical hinges — this creates a mixed stress condition unique to Miura.
  5. 5. The Miura 90° specimen is particularly interesting: it may not fail as badly as a single crease 90° specimen because the rigid-foldable property limits how much the panel face actually deforms.
→ If EXP-MO-90 survives better than EXP-SC-90, it directly proves the rigid-foldability advantage of Miura-ori for printed electronics — and that's a clean, publishable finding.

04 Pattern 2 — Parallel accordion Week 2
02 · W2
Parallel accordion — 4 creases
alternating mountain–valley · tests cumulative ΔR per crease stage
Paper & print layout
100 × 100 mm 4 creases vertical trace: parallel (vertical) 20 mm per panel
Print & fold steps
  1. 1. Cut paper to 100 × 100 mm (square).
  2. 2. Mark 4 vertical crease lines at 20, 40, 60, 80 mm from the left edge. Label alternating M (mountain) and V (valley) on the back.
  3. 3. Score all 4 creases with a stylus before printing.
  4. 4. Print traces vertically on each panel face (parallel to the vertical crease axis), centred on each 20 mm wide panel. Minimum 2 mm from crease edges. Connect panels with short bridges at alternating top/bottom edges.
  5. 5. Place contact pads on the first and last panel only.
  6. 6. Cure. Fold accordion: mountain folds fold toward you, valley folds away.
  7. 7. Measure R after each fold stage: 1 crease → 2 → 3 → 4. Then cycle test to 100.
→ The key data point here is whether ΔR is additive per crease (linear) or accelerates non-linearly. Measuring after each stage gives you that curve.

05 Pattern 3 — Miura-ori Week 2
03 · W2
Miura-ori — 2 × 2 panel grid
rigid-foldable · 1-DOF · traces on flat panels only, never on hinges
Paper & print layout
110 × 90 mm traces on panels only 2 mm clear from ALL creases 6 panel traces
Print & fold steps
  1. 1. Cut paper to 110 × 90 mm. Mark a 2 × 3 grid of panels (each ~55 × 30 mm).
  2. 2. Draw the Miura crease pattern: horizontal lines at 30 mm and 60 mm from the top, vertical lines offset by ~10 mm between rows (creating the characteristic parallelogram panels). Score all creases.
  3. 3. Print traces horizontally centred on each panel face, at least 2 mm from every crease. Do NOT let any trace cross a crease line or vertex.
  4. 4. Cure. Once cured, fold the Miura pattern: push two opposite corners together — it collapses in one motion.
  5. 5. Measure R at: fully flat → 50% folded → fully collapsed → redeployed.
  6. 6. Cycle test: fold → deploy is 1 cycle. Test at 10/25/50/100 cycles.
! The Miura has both horizontal AND vertical creases. The trace clearance rule applies to both. Check every panel on the flat layout before printing.
→ The Miura-ori is rigid-foldable — the flat panel faces do not bend at all. Your traces on those faces should survive much better than in accordion or single crease tests. This is the key insight to document.

06 Pattern 4 — Kirigami (cut + fold) Week 2 · key technique
04 · W2
Kirigami — relief cuts at crease edge
4 variants: 5 mm cuts · 10 mm cuts · 90° bridge · Kirigami-Miura (160 g/m²)
Paper & print layout
100 × 80 mm · 4 specimen groups trace: parallel (0°) or perpendicular (90°) print FIRST, cut AFTER curing
Print & fold steps
  1. 1. Score crease at horizontal centre. Print traces parallel to crease (0°) on the flat faces. Cure before cutting.
  2. 2. KR_0_5: after curing, cut 5 mm perpendicular snips at the crease edge on either side of each trace (2.5 mm each side). Valley measurement. This produced the best kirigami result: ΔR/R₀ = 0.50.
  3. 3. KR_0_10 (L1 & L2): same but 10 mm cuts (5 mm each side). Two lines cut from the same printed sample. Crease measurement. Results: ΔR/R₀ = 1.19 and 1.28 — larger cuts proved less stable.
  4. 4. KR_90_BRIDGE (L1 & L2): trace orientation 90° (perpendicular) with 40 mm bridge cuts. Valley measurement. Both specimens failed — L2 before 10 cycles, L1 before 50 cycles. Bridge geometry at perpendicular orientation is not viable.
  5. 5. KR_TWISTING: Kirigami-Miura hybrid geometry, 95 mm cuts, 160 g/m² substrate, 0° trace, crease measurement. Completed 100 cycles at ΔR/R₀ = 1.008. Not directly comparable to 80 g/m² specimens.
  6. 6. Cycle test to 100 cycles. Photograph crease zone after each checkpoint.
→ The cut must not intersect the trace. The 5 mm cut at the crease allows the fold zone to redistribute stress laterally — this is what protects the ink. Larger cuts (10 mm) introduce instability rather than relief.

07 Pattern 5 — Waterbomb base Week 3 · vertex stress study
05 · W3
Waterbomb base — valley fold study
1 specimen · WB_VALLEY · trace on triangular faces · failed >25 cycles
Paper & print layout
90 × 90 mm traces on top & bottom triangles avoid vertex and all crease lines
Print & fold steps
  1. 1. Cut paper to 90 × 90 mm (square — waterbomb requires square sheet).
  2. 2. Draw crease pattern on back: cross (vertical + horizontal centrelines) as valley folds, two diagonals as mountain folds. Score all 4 lines.
  3. 3. Print traces horizontally on the top and bottom triangular faces only, at least 15 mm from the centre vertex and 2 mm from diagonal creases. Do NOT print on left or right triangles.
  4. 4. Place contact pads at the outer edge of each trace on the top and bottom triangles.
  5. 5. Cure. Then fold the waterbomb: bring all 4 corners toward the centre, allowing the diagonals to mountain-fold. It should snap into a compressed state.
  6. 6. State 1 (flat): measure R. State 2 (collapsed): measure R. Check if the fold brings the top/bottom pad pairs into contact — if yes, you have a working bistable switch.
  7. 7. Cycle between states 10/25/50/100 times.
! The waterbomb vertex sees stress from all 4 creases simultaneously. Keep all traces and pads at least 15 mm from the centre point.

11 Pre-print checklist run before every session
Before mounting paper
  • Paper cut to exact dimensions (ruler + craft knife)
  • Crease lines scored on back with stylus before placing on bed
  • Paper flat — no curl. If curling: tape corners, or place under weight for 10 min first
  • Ink cartridge: Ailarian Sea silver ink, loaded and primed
  • Ink profile selected: Ceramics V9 in Voltera software
Before pressing print
  • Probe-map complete — height map shows surface is flat (<0.1 mm variation)
  • Gerber file: traces routed parallel to crease, 2 mm clear zone confirmed in design
  • Contact pads positioned on flat faces, not on or near crease lines
  • Print preview checked — no trace crossing any crease line
  • Specimen ID written in pencil on the back before printing starts
  • Multimeter probes ready, probe spacing jig set to 30 mm
After printing, before folding
  • Curing complete — do not fold while ink is warm or still curing
  • Visual check under magnification: traces have consistent width, no voids, no ink bleed into crease zone
  • R₀ measured and logged in Excel before any folding
  • For kirigami specimens: craft knife cuts made cleanly, not intersecting traces. Paper tested for clean fold before cycling.
  • Photo taken of flat specimen (reference for failure mode comparison later)
